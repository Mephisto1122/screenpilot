name: Draw conclusions
when: The result is a judgment, a recommendation or an answer that has to hold up, not just a list of what was found.
words: conclusion|conclude|recommend|which is better|best|should i|decide|evaluate|assess|analy[sz]e|is it true|worth
order: 7
---
- Keep three things apart in your notes: what a page says (with its address), what you saw yourself, and what you think follows.
- Support in proportion to weight: a detail, one good source; a key fact, two independent ones (not two copies of one text); a judgment of quality, examples you looked at yourself.
- Ask of every source: who says so, how would they know, when, and what do they gain.
- Look for the case against: search once for the opposite ("X problems", "X denied", the competitor's view). A conclusion that survives it is worth more.
- Numbers: note the unit, the date and what exactly was counted; compare like with like; a share needs its total.
- One example is an example. Say "of the 12 I checked, 9 ..." and not "most".
- When sources disagree, do not average: say who says what, which you trust more and why.
- Not finding something is weak evidence: say where you looked.
- State each conclusion in one sentence, then its evidence, then how sure you are (certain, likely, unclear) and what would change it.
- Never fill a gap with something plausible. "Unknown" is an answer.
