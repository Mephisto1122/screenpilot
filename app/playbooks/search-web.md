name: Search the web
when: The task needs facts, sources, prices, documents, companies or anything else that has to be found on the open web.
words: search|find|look up|research|source|price|compare|who is|what is|latest|news|review
order: 3
---
- Start from what the answer looks like on a page, not from the question: search the words the source itself would use (a job title, a product code, a phrase from a document).
- Narrow first, then wider: the exact phrase in quotes; then fewer words; then synonyms; then the language the subject lives in.
- Operators: "exact phrase"; site:domain (one site, or site:gov, site:edu); -word to drop noise; filetype:pdf for reports, price lists and decks; intitle:word; OR between alternatives; the engine's date filter for recent things.
- A site with poor search of its own: use the web search with site:.
- Read the results page before clicking: address and date show whether it is a primary source (the company, the registry, the author) or a copy. Open primary sources first; skip pages that only repeat others.
- For "best X" lists, open two or three and keep only names that appear in more than one, or that you checked yourself.
- Check the date of everything; for prices, people in roles and rules take the newest.
- Each search once. Three searches with nothing new: change the kind of source (a directory, a map, a marketplace, a forum, a registry, a platform's own search).
- A page that was removed or changed: look at web.archive.org.
- For every fact note the page address and what it says there.
