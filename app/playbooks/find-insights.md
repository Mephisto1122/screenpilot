name: Find insights
when: The user wants to understand a market, a field, competitors, an audience or a set of results: patterns and what to do about them, not a summary.
words: insight|pattern|trend|market|competitor|landscape|opportunit|gap|strategy|what works|audience|benchmark|positioning
order: 8
---
An insight is something true that the user did not know and can use. A summary is not one.
- First make the material comparable: one row per item, the same fields for each (who, what, price, size, date, source). Holes in the table are findings too.
- Then read across the rows: what the best share that the rest lack; who is the outlier and why; what changed over time; what everyone does and what nobody does; where words and numbers disagree.
- Turn counts into ratios (per follower, per month, per dollar) before comparing.
- Ask "so what, for this user's goal?" of every pattern. Keep it only if it changes what they would do.
- Be specific: "4 of the 5 with the most engagement post process videos, none of the bottom 5 do" beats "video performs well".
- Check each insight against the rows before writing it; name the rows that support it and those that do not fit.
- Give three to five, strongest first, each as: the finding, the evidence, what to do with it. Say what you could not see.
